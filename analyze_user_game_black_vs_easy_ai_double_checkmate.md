## What Made This a "Double Checkmate"?

In 9D chess, a **double checkmate** occurs when the king is in check from **multiple pieces simultaneously** with no legal escape. Here's why this is significant:

### Traditional Checkmate (2D)
- One piece attacks king
- King has no escape squares
- No piece can block or capture attacker

### Double Checkmate (9D)
- **TWO OR MORE pieces attack king simultaneously from different dimensions**
- Even if one checking piece could be captured, the other maintains check
- King cannot move, interpose, or defend due to multi-dimensional threats
- Escape squares exist in 2D plane but are covered in 3D space

### Your Final Position
```
King Position: c4z5
Checking Piece 1: Bishop on e5z6 (diagonal through z-layers)
Checking Piece 2: Bishop on a2z5 (different dimensional vector)
```

The king's escape attempts:
- **c3z5, c5z5, d4z5, d3z5** → Covered by bishop on a2z5
- **c4z4, c4z6** → Moving between z-layers doesn't escape diagonal control
- **d5z6, b3z6** → Covered by bishop on e5z6
